A checkpoint forgives. Permadeath refuses to. Roguelikes make that refusal
the entire game, and a surprising number of players in games that don't
require it turn permadeath on for themselves.

## Before the session

Bring one example of a self-imposed permadeath run — yours or someone else's
— and a guess at what it's actually for.

## In the session

We separate "consequence" from "punishment": what a roguelike run keeps
across deaths (knowledge, unlocks, meta-progress) versus what it burns, and
why that split is what makes permadeath bearable at all.

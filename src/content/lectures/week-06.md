Most games treat a death as a seam in the story: the narrative stops, a
loading screen happens, the narrative resumes as if nothing occurred. *Hades*
treats every death as the next scene — Zagreus returns home, characters
comment on the attempt, the story visibly moves forward because you failed,
not despite it.

## Outline

- narrative games' usual failure problem: repetition undercuts stakes
- how Hades' underworld setting makes death diegetic rather than an interruption
- what this technique costs — the writing overhead of scripting failure as
  content
- where the technique would break a story instead of serving it

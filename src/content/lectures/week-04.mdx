A checkpoint is an argument about how much of a mistake a player should have
to repeat. "Punishing" and "generous" aren't just adjectives here — they're
two different answers to a design question: when this hazard kills you, what
exactly does the game make you redo?

## Try it

<LiveDemo mode="checkpoint" />

Both rows hit the same hazard on the same click. The only thing that ever
differs between them is where each one wakes back up.

## Outline

- what a checkpoint is actually forgiving: time, or context, or both
- punishing saves as a design tool, not just an oversight
- generous saves and the risk of removing consequence entirely
- how this week's live demo isolates the variable from everything else in a
  real game
